What Home Credit PH commits to in return
Marketplace work is unpaid, so the exchange has to be honest. These four commitments are not goodwill — they are policy, and the People & Culture Department (PCD) is accountable for delivering each one. Not your manager, not the host manager, not you — PCD owns it centrally, which is why it holds even if the people around you change.
📁
A formal record on your file
Every completed gig, DJI, and service offer is logged permanently in your HR record with the host manager's rating — not a favour someone might remember, a document.
🎯
Inclusion in the talent review
Your marketplace contributions are tabled at the annual talent review and succession planning discussion — the room where promotions and successor slates are decided.
📝
A post-activity survey, sent immediately
The moment the engagement ends, PCD sends you the post-activity survey — what you learned, what you'd change, where you want this to lead. It goes out automatically, while the work is still fresh, and your answers route straight to PCD.
⭐
First look at related roles
Complete work in a function and you are flagged as a priority internal candidate when a vacancy opens there. Proven contribution counts ahead of a fresh application.
On work hours and workload. Gigs are done outside office hours, or during office hours only with your manager's written permission. DJIs follow an agreed role split under Policy 211_2021. Your primary role and KPIs are never expected to absorb the difference — if a marketplace commitment starts to compromise your core work, raise it with your manager or PCD and it will be adjusted or ended. No one is penalised for that conversation.

✨ My Matches
Opportunities ≥ 70% match with your current skills
🤖
These matches are generated by AI
Claude
Nobody sits and ranks you against every post. When a new opportunity goes live, an AI model reads your profile — your skills, your stated aspiration, your marketplace history — against what the post actually asks for, and returns a score with its reasoning. You can read that reasoning on every card, and you can disagree with it: the score never blocks an application. There is deliberately no minimum to apply.
📊 How the score is calculated — the full rubric
Four factors are weighted, then banded. Verified skills count roughly double a self-claimed one.
FactorWeightWhat the model looks at
Skill overlap50%How many of the required skills you hold, and whether each is verified (manager endorsement, a 4★+ host rating, or an LMS certification) or self-claimed.
Aspiration fit20%Whether the work moves you toward the direction you wrote on your profile — not just what you can already do.
Track record20%Completed gigs, DJIs and service offers, and the ratings host managers gave you.
Practicalities10%Bandwidth asked for, duration, and department — a signal, never a disqualifier.
BandReads asWhat we suggest
85–100%Strong matchYou can do this now. Apply.
70–84%Good matchOne or two gaps, closable on the job. Apply.
50–69%StretchA real step up. Worth applying if the direction excites you — say so in your answer.
Below 50%Long shotApply if you're genuinely drawn to it, and use the development suggestions on the card.
Where it can be wrong. The model only knows what is written down. A skill you never added, or experience recorded nowhere, is invisible to it — which is why a thin profile produces low scores that say more about the profile than the person. It also cannot judge motivation. If a score looks wrong to you, it probably is: apply anyway, and tell the post owner why.

🎯 Referrals & Attribution
How many people the marketplace sent to HC Connect Internal Job Posting — and how much of that anyone can prove
What each number is worth
Measured here Views and referrals happen inside this app. They are exact, and they are the marketplace's own contribution — nobody can dispute them.
Self-reported The employee told us they applied. Useful and free, but it flatters: people who applied are likelier to answer than people who did not. Quote it as self-reported or not at all.
From HC Connect A row in the HC Connect export carries the referral code, or a source value of Growth Marketplace. This is the only figure to put in a paper that goes to Exco.
Monthly reconciliation — the whole runbook
1. Export referrals from this page. One row per handoff, with the referral code, the requisition, the person and the date.
2. Ask recruitment for the HC Connect export of internal applications for the same period, including the source field and the free-text "how did you hear" answer.
3. Join on the referral code. Where there is no code, fall back to matching on employee plus requisition, and count it only if the referral click precedes the application by no more than 30 days.
4. Rows that join are confirmed. Rows that do not are not evidence of anything — the person may have applied without pasting the code, or may never have applied. Leave them out; do not estimate them upward.
5. Once a quarter, ask HR for hire outcomes on the confirmed applications. That is the only attributable hire number.
✅ One change in HC Connect makes all of this cheap. Ask recruitment to add a source value Growth Marketplace and one optional free-text field for the referral code. That is a configuration change on a form, not an integration — and it turns the reconciliation from a name-matching exercise into a join on a key.
The honest limit: attribution is not causation
A confirmed referral proves the marketplace was the last thing the person touched before applying. It does not prove they would not have applied anyway — some of them read the same role in the careers newsletter that morning.

If someone challenges the number, there is exactly one clean answer: run a holdout. For one quarter, promote a random half of open requisitions on the marketplace and leave the other half listed only in HC Connect. Compare internal applications per requisition across the two groups. The difference is the marketplace's causal effect, and it is the only version of this number that survives a hostile reading. It costs nothing but the discipline to leave half the reqs alone.

Until that runs, report the funnel as it is labelled: referrals are ours, applications are HC Connect', and the word "because" belongs to neither of us yet.

❓ FAQ
Frequently asked questions about Talent Marketplace

For Employees

Will joining a gig or immersion affect my KPIs or salary? +
No. Participation is ad-hoc and voluntary. Your core role, KPIs, and compensation remain unchanged. Per Policy 211_2021, an employee undergoing DJI is not eligible for incentives, bonuses, or allowances specific to the host department — and no additional bonuses are provided for being in a DJI. Your headcount stays in your home department throughout.
What are the work hours arrangements? +
For Gigs: Gig work should be done outside regular office hours, or during office hours only with your manager's written permission. Your primary role and KPIs must not be disrupted.

For Developmental Job Immersions (DJI) — per Policy 211_2021, the degree of immersion is agreed upon and documented in the DJI Plan before the immersion begins:
  • Full immersion (100%) — you are fully released to the Host Department for the duration
  • Partial immersion — the new role is performed alongside your current role at an agreed split

This is co-designed by you, your Direct Manager, and the Host Manager, then signed off by all three plus your HR People Partner (HRPP) using the official DJI Form.
📄 Policy 211_2021 v1.0 — Section 5.4.2 Goal and Expectation Setting
Who is eligible to join? +
For Gigs and Vacancies: Most employees in good standing are eligible. Not eligible if on a PIP or performance rating of 2 or lower in the last cycle.

For DJI specifically (per Policy 211_2021), you must meet all of:
  • Regular employee of the company
  • At least 12 months in your current position
  • No disciplinary action in the last 12 months
  • PIP/underperformers reviewed on a case-by-case basis with HRPP
📄 Policy 211_2021 v1.0 — Section 5.1 Eligibility
There's no extra pay — so what does the company actually give me in return? +
A fair question, and the honest answer is that "exposure" isn't payment. Marketplace work is unpaid, so Home Credit PH commits to four things in exchange. These are policy, not goodwill, and the People & Culture Department (PCD) is accountable for delivering each — centrally, not devolved to your manager:

1. A formal record on your file. Every completed engagement is logged permanently in your HR record with the host manager's rating. Not a favour someone might remember — a document.
2. Inclusion in the talent review. Your contributions are tabled at the annual talent review and succession planning discussion — the room where promotions and successor slates are decided.
3. A post-activity survey, immediately. The moment the engagement ends, PCD sends you the post-activity survey — what you learned, what you'd change, where you want it to lead. Sent automatically while the work is fresh, and it goes to PCD, not into a drawer.
4. First look at related roles. Complete work in a function and you're flagged as a priority internal candidate when a vacancy opens there.

On workload. Your primary role and KPIs are never expected to absorb the difference. If a marketplace commitment starts compromising your core work, raise it with your manager or HRPP — it will be adjusted or ended, and no one is penalised for that conversation.

Beyond the commitments, the reasons people actually give: fulfilling your IDP, a creative outlet, genuine love of the work, test-driving a career pivot with zero risk, visibility with leaders outside your chain, a cross-functional network that compounds for your whole career, and a shorter learning curve when you do move roles.
Can I nominate someone else instead of applying myself? +
Yes — and please do. Every opportunity in All Opportunities has a 🤝 Nominate button beside Apply.

Why it matters. Nominated candidates are selected at roughly 41% against 23% for self-applications. Someone who has seen the work vouching for a specific person is a far stronger signal than a self-assessment. Nomination is also how the marketplace reaches the people who would never put themselves forward — often exactly the people who should.

Especially for managers. You see your team's capability more clearly than they do. Nominating a team member is one of the highest-leverage things you can do in this platform: it costs you nothing, it signals to them that you back their growth, and it's tracked. Managers whose nominees get selected are recognised at the quarterly Exco review.

How to nominate well. You must say why they're the best applicant, and specificity is what gets picked up. "She rebuilt our reconciliation model in two weeks and trained the team on it" lands; "she'd be great" doesn't.

The nominee stays in control. They're notified, they see your reason, and they can accept or decline. Nobody is entered into anything without their consent.
What is a Service Offer, and how is it different? +
It's the one type that works in reverse. Instead of applying for work someone else has posted, you publish what you're willing to help with and teams come to you.

Typical offers: mentoring on product and customer segments, vibe-coding a quick prototype, reviewing a pricing model, coaching a first-time presenter, walking someone through a system you know well.

You stay in control. You set your own capacity (say, up to 4 hours a month), accept only the requests that fit, and pause the offer any time. Most are an hour or two at a time.

Why bother. Every completed request is logged on your profile with a rating. It's the fastest way to build an internal reputation for what you're genuinely good at — and the same four company commitments above apply.

Set one up under My Profile → What I can help others with.
How are skills on my profile verified? +
Anyone can claim a skill, so claims alone don't carry much weight. A skill becomes ✅ Verified three ways:

1. Manager endorsement. Your direct manager confirms it. Request this from your profile in one click.
2. Marketplace performance. A host manager rates you 4★ or higher on work that used the skill — it's verified automatically. This is the strongest form, because it's evidence rather than opinion.
3. Certification. A completed certification logged in the LMS.

Verified skills rank higher in matching and are the only ones shown to post owners by default. Self-declared skills still count — they just carry a lighter weight until something backs them up. Over time, completed marketplace work becomes the main source of truth about what people here can actually do.
What happens after an engagement ends? +
Two weeks after the end date on the post, both you and the other party are automatically asked for a short rating and comment. Under two minutes.

Ratings release simultaneously. Neither of you sees the other's until both are in, so nobody is anchored by what the other said.

The rating and comment land on your permanent record, feed skill verification, and are visible in the talent review. PCD then sends you the post-activity survey immediately, so your own account of the work is captured while you still remember it.

This is the mechanism that turns unpaid work into something that actually counts. Without it, the company's commitments would be a promise with nothing behind them — so the feedback step isn't optional for either side.
Will my manager see that I'm exploring opportunities? +
You control your visibility. Expressing interest in gigs is discreet — your manager is not automatically notified. DJIs require manager approval as part of the process, so they will be involved.
What happens after I express interest? +
The opportunity owner is notified and will review your profile. They may invite you to a conversation, accept, or decline. You will be notified either way.

For Managers & HR

Why should I allow my team member to apply? +
Beyond the right thing to do, here's the business case:

Retention. Employees who feel stuck leave. Giving someone a growth outlet within the company costs nothing — keeping them costs far less than replacing them (estimated 50–200% of annual salary per replacement).

Engagement. People who stretch become more engaged, not less. They return to their core role with new energy, new perspectives, and conviction that you care about their growth.

Bench strength. A broader-skilled team member is more valuable to your team. They can cross-cover and bring back capabilities you don't have to hire for.

Employer brand. Managers who support mobility are the ones top talent wants to work for.

The math. For gigs and DJIs at partial capacity, you still have 50–80% of your team member for the duration. Most managers find this workable — especially versus losing the person entirely.

Go further: nominate them. Allowing someone to apply is passive. Putting their name forward yourself is the strongest development signal a manager can send — it says you see their capability and you're willing to spend your credibility on it. Your nominations and their outcomes are tracked, and managers whose nominees get selected are recognised at the quarterly Exco review.
Why does Growth show vacancies if I can't apply for them here? +
Because finding the role and applying for it are two different problems, and only one of them was ever broken.

HC Connect is good at running an application. It is not good at telling you that a role exists in a department you never look at, for which you happen to be a 90% skills match. That is what Growth does — and it is the reason a permanent role you would never have searched for ends up in front of you.

What the referral code is for. When you open a vacancy from here, we generate a code like GRW-K7P2QX and pass it on the link. Paste it into the "How did you hear about this role?" field in HC Connect. It is the only evidence that connects your application back to the marketplace — without it, the recruitment report shows an application from nowhere and this page looks like it does nothing.

What it is not. It is not tracking your application, it does not follow you into HC Connect, and nobody sees your CV through Growth. The code carries which role you opened and that you came from here. Nothing else.
What's the difference between a gig, a vacancy, and a DJI? +
Gig: Short-term project, a few hours to a few weeks, done part-time. Outside office hours or with written manager permission.

Vacancy: Open permanent role — a formal internal move to a new team. You do not apply for these in Growth. Vacancies are run in HC Connect Internal Job Posting, the recruitment system. Growth lists them, scores your fit, and hands you over on a tracked link; everything after that — CV, screening, interviews, offer — happens in HC Connect.

Developmental Job Immersion (DJI) per Policy 211_2021: Temporary assignment to another role or function, 3 to 9 months. Two types:
  • For Development — gaining exposure/experience for learning; no open requisition for the target role
  • For Job Consideration — assessing readiness for the role; open requisition exists

In both types, the employee's headcount, salary, and benefits remain with their home department. No additional allowances or bonuses are provided.

Service Offer: The fourth type, and the only one that works in reverse — an employee publishes what they're willing to help with (mentoring, a prototype, a review) and teams request their time. Usually an hour or two at a stretch, capacity set by the person offering.
📄 Policy 211_2021 v1.0 — Sections 5.2, 5.3, 5.4
What if a team member wants to join a DJI opportunity? +
Per Policy 211_2021, you are a core part of the process:

1. You and the employee, in consultation with the HRPP, agree the DJI is part of their Individual Development Plan
2. You co-design the DJI Plan with the employee and Host Manager — covering goals, degree of immersion, expected outputs, and performance evaluation impact
3. You, the employee, the Host Manager, and the HRPP all sign off using the official DJI Form
4. As Direct Manager, you remain responsible for check-ins, evaluation, and formal closure of the DJI

The employee's headcount and all HR obligations remain with you throughout. The DJI closes at the agreed end date, or at 9 months maximum.
📄 Policy 211_2021 v1.0 — Sections 4 & 5.4
How long does it take to post an opportunity? +
Under 5 minutes. Fill in the type, title, department, required skills, duration, and available slots — then submit. The opportunity enters an HR admin review queue before going live.
Do I have to take whoever the system matches? +
No. Matching is a suggestion, not an assignment. You review interested applicants and decide who to accept, invite to a conversation, or decline. The final decision is always yours.
What's expected after the exposure ends? +
Both the post owner and the accepted participant complete a short feedback form — a star rating (1–5) and optional comment. Under 2 minutes, and it helps improve the platform over time.
